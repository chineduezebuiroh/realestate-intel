# C1.2 Decision Outputs v1

## Purpose

This document defines the decisions the Regime Engine is intended to support.

The regime system is designed as a decision-support framework rather than a purely descriptive analytics framework.

Every regime output should contribute to at least one decision defined in this document.

---

# Primary Decision Categories

The Regime Engine supports five primary decision categories:

1. Development Decisions
2. Acquisition Decisions
3. Hold / Exit Decisions
4. Market Comparison Decisions
5. Forward-Looking Decisions

---

# Development Decisions

Primary Question:

> Is this market supportive of new development activity?

Relevant Signals:

* Demand strength
* Supply pressure
* Affordability conditions
* Capital availability

Example Outputs:

* Favorable
* Neutral
* Unfavorable

The regime engine does not replace underwriting.

The objective is to identify whether market conditions are broadly supportive of development activity.

---

# Acquisition Decisions

Primary Question:

> Is this market attractive for acquisition?

Relevant Signals:

* Cycle position
* Demand momentum
* Liquidity
* Price behavior

Example Outputs:

* Attractive
* Selective
* Defensive

The goal is not to identify individual properties but to evaluate market-level attractiveness.

---

# Hold / Exit Decisions

Primary Question:

> Should market exposure be maintained, increased, or reduced?

Relevant Signals:

* Cycle maturity
* Transition risk
* Demand deterioration
* Supply acceleration

Example Outputs:

* Increase Exposure
* Maintain Exposure
* Reduce Exposure

---

# Market Comparison Decisions

Primary Question:

> How does this market compare to alternative markets?

Examples:

* Washington vs Baltimore
* Washington vs Atlanta
* County A vs County B

The Regime Engine should enable direct comparison across markets regardless of size.

---

# Forward-Looking Decisions

Primary Question:

> Where is this market likely heading?

This output is generated by applying the Regime Engine to forecasted indicators rather than current indicators.

Examples:

* Current Regime = Expansion
* Forecast Regime = Late Expansion

or

* Current Regime = Recovery
* Forecast Regime = Expansion

---

# Risk Signaling

In addition to regime labels, the engine should surface:

* confidence score
* transition risk
* data coverage quality

This helps users distinguish between:

* strong signals
* weak signals
* uncertain classifications

---

# Non-Goals

The Regime Engine is not intended to:

* recommend individual property purchases
* generate buy/sell signals
* replace underwriting
* replace human judgment

The Regime Engine provides market context for decision-making.

---

# Success Criteria

The Regime Engine is successful if it helps users make:

* better development decisions
* better acquisition decisions
* better market selection decisions

while reducing the amount of underlying data required for interpretation.
