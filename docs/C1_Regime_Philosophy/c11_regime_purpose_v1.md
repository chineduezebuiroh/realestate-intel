# C1.1 Regime Purpose v1

## Purpose

This document defines the purpose of the Real Estate Regime Engine and establishes the role the regime system plays within the broader realestate-intel platform.

This document intentionally focuses on philosophy rather than implementation.

Questions such as:

* cycle-wheel construction
* axis calculations
* state boundaries
* forecasting methodology

are addressed in later documents.

---

# What Is A Regime?

A regime is a standardized representation of a market's current position within the real estate cycle.

The purpose of a regime is to compress large amounts of economic, housing, affordability, liquidity, pricing, and capital-market information into a small set of interpretable market states.

A regime is not intended to replace the underlying data.

Instead, it serves as a decision layer that translates many indicators into an understandable description of market conditions.

---

# Why Regimes Exist

Real estate markets generate hundreds of indicators across many different data sources.

Examples include:

* employment growth
* unemployment trends
* GDP growth
* inventory growth
* permitting activity
* affordability metrics
* liquidity metrics
* mortgage rates
* yield spreads
* housing prices

Individually, these indicators provide useful information.

Collectively, they become difficult to interpret consistently.

The regime engine exists to transform these indicators into a coherent representation of market conditions.

---

# Core Objective

The primary objective of the regime engine is to answer:

> Where is this market currently positioned within the real estate cycle?

The engine should provide a consistent answer regardless of:

* geography
* property type
* time period
* data source

---

# Decision-Oriented Philosophy

The regime engine is designed to support decision-making rather than purely descriptive analytics.

The engine should help users evaluate:

* development opportunities
* acquisition opportunities
* hold / disposition decisions
* market selection decisions
* portfolio allocation decisions

The regime system is therefore intended to be actionable rather than merely explanatory.

---

# Decision Outputs

The regime engine should ultimately support five primary decisions.

## Development Decision

Question:

> Is this market supportive of new development activity?

The answer should reflect:

* demand conditions
* supply conditions
* affordability conditions
* capital availability

---

## Acquisition Decision

Question:

> Is this market attractive for acquisition?

The answer should reflect:

* market momentum
* cycle position
* expected future conditions

---

## Hold / Exit Decision

Question:

> Should exposure to this market be maintained, increased, or reduced?

The answer should reflect:

* current conditions
* cycle maturity
* transition risk

---

## Market Selection Decision

Question:

> How does this market compare to alternative markets?

The answer should support cross-market comparison rather than absolute judgment.

---

## Forward-Looking Decision

Question:

> Where is this market likely heading?

The regime system should support forecast-based regime analysis using forecasted indicator values generated by the forecasting engine.

---

# Relationship To Forecasting

The regime engine is not itself a forecasting system.

The forecasting engine and regime engine are separate layers.

The forecasting engine generates future indicator values.

The regime engine consumes those values and applies the same scoring, coordinate, and classification framework used for current market conditions.

This allows the platform to produce:

* current regime
* forecast regime
* expected regime transitions

using a single regime framework.

---

# Continuous Markets, Discrete Labels

Markets move continuously.

Regimes are discrete labels imposed on a continuous process.

For example:

* Demand Axis = 0.72
* Supply Axis = -0.41

represent a continuous market position.

A regime label such as:

* Expansion
* Recovery
* Hypersupply

is simply a classification assigned to that position.

This distinction is important because market conditions evolve gradually while regime labels change only when classification boundaries are crossed.

---

# Macro Versus Local Regimes

The platform supports both macro and local regime analysis.

## Macro Regime

Macro regimes describe the broader market cycle.

Examples:

* CBSA
* County

Macro regimes capture the dominant economic and housing-cycle forces affecting a market.

---

## Local Regime

Local regimes describe specific market segments operating within a broader market.

Examples:

* Neighborhood
* ZIP code
* Property type

Local regimes may differ materially from the surrounding macro regime.

Examples:

* Washington Metro = Expansion
* Deanwood = Recovery

or

* Washington Metro = Late Expansion
* Luxury Condos = Hypersupply

Both may be true simultaneously.

---

# Non-Goals

The regime engine is not intended to:

* predict exact prices
* predict exact timing of turning points
* replace underlying indicators
* function as a trading signal
* eliminate the need for market-specific judgment

The regime engine is a decision-support framework.

---

# Success Criteria

The regime engine is successful if it can:

* consistently classify market conditions
* explain market position intuitively
* support investment decision-making
* support market comparison
* support forecast-based scenario analysis
* remain interpretable by humans

without requiring users to inspect dozens of underlying indicators.
